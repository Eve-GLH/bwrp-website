The Bard's attempt to define Moju by Lenjamin T Pondswhistle

Foreword

One should understand that history often stretches beyond what words may capture. Though the lives of mortals may feel long and ponderous, in the eyes of the oldest mountains or the deepest seas the life of a single creature may be but a single beat of prose. Do not ascribe equal time to every line or event in this writing, for a single passage might describe centuries with the same amount of words as one may describe a single moment.

So too should the reader brace themselves for the myriad definitions of spirits and spirituals. Here shall be discussed neither, but instead a form of being that exists upon the threshold, a shepherd between material and immaterial, a heartbeat unheard and unmoved; the very life essence of the lands themselves.

The term Moju has meant much to so many, from the numbered Mojudan faiths, to the first feet that trod upon great hills. Regardless, these pages shall attempt to define it, not in chains but through the tales of those most noted three to bear such a title.

Within, we shall unveil normalcy, greatness, and ends most foul.

Faroch, the First to Pass

Though the spirit of the Hills and Rainforests of the Faroch Dreich is one of the oldest Moju found in written memory, he was not the First Moju.

Long before the Endless Spears or Hjen arrived in the Venswood, before the Kihai first climbed from the soil, the First Cuthail gave offerings to Faroch, the supposed spirit of the hill they made their homes within. They built shrines, offering stones, as they had done to the Once Gods, eager now to turn their gaze from their old masters' twisting ways.

With this tribute, Faroch stirred, and in return for the kindness of the Cuthail, gave them most fertile land that they might grow their food upon. And so the Cuthail planted fields of wheat, and with food, their number began to grow. And as the Cuthail spread, so too did the influence of Faroch, as they continued to give offering and tribute to Faroch the hill.

There was goodness in the land. The seasons were as kind as they may be.

As time passed, and the telling of Faroch changed, he became not only of the hill, but hills, but then the forests upon them and then too the forests at their foot as well. The Cuthail prospered under him, in the hills and groves, and spread further and further, believing more and more, knowing not that this would spell the end of Faroch in some countless generations.

Faroch, grown larger than any other Moju had been before, saw now that those that knew of him had spread so far, so separated, that the stories, the beliefs that had first stirred him now differed so much that it spread him thin. Despite this, he continued to tend to those in his care, growing fainter and fainter, until he could no longer be distinguished from the simple turnings of the world, then fainter still until he was returned to the land that he was born from. In his place now, countless tales of rivers, trees, wolfpacks and mighty herds, each bearing Faroch's blessing and boon, but each now with their own names, and their own gifts to bestow.

And so, not as some sad ending but some great tectonic life lived, and purpose fulfilled; Faroch became the First Moju to Pass.

Behemoth, the Torn

While Faroch was the Greatest Moju of his time, the Greatest Moju in all of history without a doubt, was Behemoth. Where Faroch came to the same end that most Greater Moju have, Behemoth was held together, through great belief and dedication from those that followed him.

An aeon passed under his guidance, the lands beneath him flourishing into the grand cornucopia of life that the Venswood later became known for as he encompassed it all and countless lands beyond. He grew in subtle ways to protect his denizens from unseen threats, and when the ancestors of the Endless Spears came, to learn how to protect themselves, he taught them how to strike both material and immaterial with bone, thorn, and bond.

The land thrived, and the Cuthail walked among the roots and wanted for little.

But, it was not to last. Behemoth saw the Sundering approach; slow, reaching, but inevitable in its movements. He saw the splitting of the world under two skies, and he knew what this meant. While lesser Moju may survive the ordeal, swept with one half of the world or the other, one so encompassing and magnanimous as he would not shift. A world Sundered would be a Behemoth torn; ideas do not live half lives.

The Sundered tides of aether would change what it means to be a Moju. To be immaterial in a world now separated from the Other, they could act little beyond tilting the usual turnings of the world, but this was their way and, none other than Behemoth would fear themselves split.

Rather than meet his halving end, Behemoth chose to let go of himself, of his power, of all but his care. Choosing instead to seed new life in what would then become the Venswood as his final act; so that he may go on in many small ways, and perhaps come to see the world as his favoured students did.

Amatl, the Slain

Where Behemoth chose to let go of himself, and most other Moju chose to slumber and continue their works as the Sundering tore the World, some Moju chose to step beyond their natural role, in a bid to avoid the perceived shackles of the Sundering.

One such Moju was Amatl. Not eager to face the new reality awaiting the Moju, already straining the definition of the word, he hatched a plan to become something New.

In the vacuum of influence left by Behemoth’s leaving, he began gathering followers, so that he may tie himself to more than the land, asking offerings, tithes, and sacrifice to grow his power enough to hold his form whole in this new half-world. He promised prosperity to those that followed him, and while they did receive it, it was a trade that those in the path of his expansionism paid for.

His desperate clawing for survival would see him consume nearby spirits, and the fervour he had already instilled in those that followed him carried them to consume likewise. His dominion spread outwards, the people taking more, pressing away from the heart of the domain in their own desperation. For at the heart of such a thing, where He now touched the mortal world the world bent to whim rather than nature.

Battles were fought, lines drawn and drawn again, but what hope had mortalkind against beings beyond?

Despite it all, his reign was destined to be short-lived, as his etheocracy, his breaking of the natural order, did not go unnoticed. The Endless Spears, with a primal fury never seen before and only once since, arrived in the Venswood and slew Amatl and his sycophants with that craft which Behemoth taught them.

While this returned the natural balance to the Venswood, the heart of Amatl’s domain became barren and lifeless, for all that remained there were bones, and the reminder to any of the beyond who would dare to challenge the natural order and make slaves of mortalkind.

Afterwards

The true definition of a Moju is as wide as the seas and even deeper than the Earth. For a sole definition to be used it would be a mistreatment of the long and storied history of them.

These great tales were from a different, primordial, age. A world now far removed from what still turns. Even though these legends lived so far from us, their lessons remain true for what it means to be that kind of being and bear the name Moju.

Even things that seem timeless turn in their own cycle.
