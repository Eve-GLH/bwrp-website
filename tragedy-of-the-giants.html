The Tragedy of the Giants

The Bigger They Are, The Harder They Fall

The Giants were a magical race of beings from aeons past, gargantuan in size, and magical aptitude. Their societies flourished through grand magicks, and in doing so, use of the Weave was commonplace in their everyday lives.

When the Sundering split the World in two, taking magic with it, the Giants’s civilization suffered the most of all the material kins, for like other great beings, they were unable to survive without both their halves. The living giants began to lose their ability to shape the Weave so adeptly, but even more catastrophic was the effects it had on their descendants. Each generation that followed had a lesser hold on the Weave, and smaller stature to match. These lesser generations had to rely on the Giants of Old to protect them, and found themselves unable to remain self-sufficient in a society that they could not truly access without magic.

The first of these generations could not bear to live in the shadow of the ruins of Giant society. Forced both to look up at their forefathers and their great works, and down upon their later born brothers they lost hope in their reality and fled the land, taking to the seas. Those that remained would eventually become the Ettins of today.

For the Giants Of Old, despite their undying stature, their hold on magic lessened; leaving more and more to be lost to the dangers of their homeland over the aeons. Unable to sculpt true Giant children any longer, the population of Giants began to dwindle.

Tangiata & Manawaika

When their paths first crossed at the Last Great Convocation of the Giants, Tangiata & Manawaika were certainly not a tale of love at first sight. Truth be told, neither much liked the other at all on that day, as the Giants gathered to talk about the Crisis that the Sundering brought upon their people.

The first lesser descendants having fled the lands already, the Giants of Old found themselves locked in fierce debate over the descendants that remained, and the responsibility that they held to them. Many argued over the duty held to these beings they had created, while others refuted that putting care for these Ettins -something little more than a failed creation- above attempting to save Giant Society would only spell further disaster for their peoples.

A great many years passed before Tangiata & Manawaika would meet again. Manawaika was the caretaker of an Ettin town, keeping them safe from the many threats of Nebelloren, especially the Dragons that had taken to the weakened Giants like birds to a worm. Tangiata had walked away from his kin, instead trying -and failing- to restore one of the Great Giant works that the Sundering had brought to ruin.

Fleeing from one such dragon, Tangiata found himself in need of aid, and found his salvation when Manawaika and the strongest of her flock slew the dragon and brought Tangiata to their town to recover. Once he was well, Tangiata thanked Manawaika, but refused her offer to stay, returning to the wilds alone.

Over the following aeon, Tangiata & Manawaika would cross paths from time to time, frequently coming to the others aid, each time growing ever closer, and each time mourning more and more of their kin, as the Giants of Old dwindled in number, taken by the dangers of the land, some through Noble action, some through desperation.

Eventually, only two Giants remained. They found each other in their grief, and forged a bond that would carry them through the years and years, with only the other to keep their company. Under Manawaika’s watch and care, the Ettins may not have flourished, but they did survive.

When the Syzygy finally began, and the world began to pull itself together once more, the two Oldest Giants felt the Weave at their fingertips once more, like it had aeons and aeons before. And with these newly returned magicks, they created the first Giant child since the Sundering, which they named Linnéa.

The End of the Giants

Linnéa was but entering her second century when the first washups came ashore. First created just large enough for a giant to hold between finger and thumb, she had grown into a being perhaps just larger than a Hjen and was often trusted to wander the lands around the Great Eastern Wyrmwoods on her own. For the first True Giant in an epoch the roaming magics and wild things were of no concern and most hardly paid her any attention.

But the woods had a new predator. The Kihai leaders of the Choir had set their aim to tame this wood, having made camp below it already. Some of their number alongside the Spears of their camp set to calling out to the ancient spirits of the Otherside bringing the Phoenix's gaze upon Nebelloren and its fist down upon the local Gnoll tribes.

Both of her parents being absent, Manawaika to meet with the remaining Ettins of the land, Tangiata deep below beginning restoration on one of the Great Giant works of Old, pushed Linnéa to act alone. A self decided protector of the woods, she thought to smother their ritual fire and crept atop the hills by their town, Cadence, before hurling boulders down at the great flames.

Chaos ensued. A visiting Dwarven doctor, Mirabel 'Twista' Arelbel led a response, taking a hunting party from both the Choir and Entente to hunt down this threat from the woods.

Linnéa evaded them, making it back to their home in the nearby mountains but Twista was relentless. Half the hunting party departed but still she drove on, eventually finding Linnéa not through some feat of huntsmanship or forestcraft but simple determination to take every step in the woods until her quarry was found.

There was no fight nor battle, only a swift quiet death in a humble cave.

Manawaika returned to her home-turned-pyre and wept for a season before using her tears to scry the culprit.

It was she that then entreated the Gnollish King; Brenin Hen Aelwyd, her grief that spurred the first siege of Parravon where she too would fall. Brought low by bow, axe and blood magics in Balink's field.

Her form was butchered. Blood drained, thumbs severed and skull taken. Her blood was dripped over countless runes. Her thumbs whittled to become arms of a bow. Her skull, first used as a trophy for the tourney ground, became the focal point for the land's most powerful rituals.

Tangiata heard no tale for years. His works at restoration robbed him of any farewells, serving to keep him far from even grief. Unlike Manawaika, he was no proud warrior, knew little of the struggles of the peoples of the land. He marched on Cadence alone, then all but abandoned, he tore down all he could find. Then, he walked the width of the land to find Parravon and do the same. It was only chance he found the city besieged once again when he arrived. The gnolls had pressed the Entente back behind their walls but those walls bristled with ballistae and with most loaded poor Tangiata was felled in the great fields across from the keep.

There his bones rested for five years before he was raised again to bring down Parravon's walls.

What happened to the Giants? Why are they all gone?

There are no giants because of the follies of themselves, their kin, would be gods and lesser beings.

They are now what ruins and bones still mark the earth where they fell, dust and memory, as all things become.
