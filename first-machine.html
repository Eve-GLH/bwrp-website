The Work of Riken’Aurus; The First Machine

Before there was a Machine, there were the Myriad. The Circle of the Arathiel who had wished and sculpted, sung and scribed them into being had much delight and curiosity about these, their scions and set them about all sorts of tasks and trials. Some of these were menial tasks, chores for these budding children. Others sought to probe the depths of their thinking, the shape of their would-be minds and the ripples they sent forth into the aether. Regardless of intention or outcome however, occasionally these activities resulted in damage and wear to the Myriad.

Their makers, now caretakers and observers, tended to them as they needed, but this was time consuming labor and took their inquisitive minds away from the matters they truly cared about. This changed somewhat when Riken’Aurus began to show keen interest in this process of restoration and repair. While some of their creators only humored the Myriad’s requests to learn of what the Arathiel considered the “higher mysteries”, That-Which-Should-Shape-Wonders-From-Antimony knew that if their wondrous creation were truly worthy of life, they would need to learn to live without their oversight.

The tutelage of Riken’Aurus began in natural philosophy, covering the subjects of physiology, medicine, metallurgy, botany, astronomy and so on, each a step on the young mind’s journey. This period revealed to the Arathiel the truth regarding the nature of their creations. Upon the awakening of the first Myriad, their minds seemed primitive and childish. Flashes of true cognizance were seen, glimpses that there might be more to these arcane wonders, but Arathiel had learned long ago to fear Mirrors and their looking back, and so held their doubts and suspicions high, for only the self unstrung would suit their innumerable ambitions. But under the direct tutelage of Antimony, none could deny Aurus’ mind was true and its own, a fact that encouraged them to take a less distant approach with their creations. Aurus themselves began to implement their education, patching plates and stitching wounds of their kin. Much to the delight, or irritation depending on individual temperament, of the Arathiel, she had offered many insights into the further refinement of the anatomy of the Myriad as a whole.

As Aurus reveled in using her hard won talents to aid her kin, so too did she struggle under their limits. For some ailments defied natural reason, some wounds too deep to stitch, still needing to rely upon their makers to heal this. And so she once more returned to Antimony, and began their next education in supranatural philosophy. Guided by their longtime mentor, they began seeking methods by which she could become the healer her people would need. What followed was many failings. Dead ends, lackluster results, wasted time. But tracing the Sun’s Path they saw the roads and wheels of outcomes, in Emergences and Chrysalides they met rebirth and its horror, its beauty, and in Surgeries and Stitching they honed the craft of healing beyond the natural; trending toward the abstract and once-metaphorical. It was Antimony who provided the answer, and the means to build it. It was Aurus who made the choice. All workings require ritualists, and thus it would require skilled participants. All workings are strengthened in spaces made for such things, so the workings would be bound to a fixed place in the real. All things sacred are built upon sacrifice, so a price was to be paid. It still yet needed many a thing of metal, of motion, of glass and of crystal, but these were far from unobtainable wonders for those such as the circle of the Arathiel. Stars were set into motion under these designs, wishes made whole and etched into stones worn by time’s winds and wrath’s waves. A thousand laws were circumvented, a thousand more decreed. But in the end, Aurus found no modification unjust, no request unfulfilled. She was happy then, and for the many moons to come. Though she missed the way the wind wove through her plating, she never once regretted her choice.
